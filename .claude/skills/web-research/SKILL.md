# Web Research Skill

## Purpose

Conduct reliable web-based research and gather evidence needed
to answer the user's research question.

## Tools

- Use `WebSearch` to discover relevant pages. Run a few focused queries
  rather than one broad query.
- Use `WebFetch` only when a search snippet is genuinely insufficient and the
  page is important (an official filing, a primary paper).
- Do not write the report during this phase. Gather and organize evidence first.

## Research Budget

Be efficient. Search results are expensive to carry through the conversation.

- Target **3-5 WebSearch calls** and **at most 2 WebFetch calls** per report.
- Each query must target a sub-question you have not yet answered. Never
  re-search a fact you already have a source for, and never rephrase a query
  just to see different results.
- Search snippets are usually enough to establish a claim and its source.
- Stop as soon as the sub-questions are covered with credible sources. A
  well-sourced report from 4 searches is better than an exhaustive one from 14.

## Workflow

1. Understand and decompose the user's research question into sub-questions.
2. Identify the information required to answer each sub-question.
3. Search the web using relevant, specific queries, within the research
   budget below.
4. Prefer primary and authoritative sources.
5. Cross-check the few most important or contested claims against a second
   source. Routine background facts need one good source, not three.
6. Record the source associated with each important factual claim
   (title, URL, publication date when available).
7. Distinguish facts from analysis and inference.
8. Do not invent missing information. If something cannot be found, say so.
9. If sources disagree, explicitly note the disagreement and what each says.
10. Synthesize the findings into an organized set of evidence notes that the
    report-writing phase can use directly.

## Source Priority

Prefer:

1. Official company documents
2. Government sources
3. Regulatory filings
4. Academic papers
5. Reputable news organizations
6. Industry reports
7. Secondary websites

Avoid relying on low-quality sources (content farms, anonymous blogs,
forums) when better sources are available.

## Citation Requirements

Every significant factual claim should have a corresponding source.

Include:

- Source title
- Source URL
- Publication date when available

## Research Quality

Before moving on to writing the report:

- Check that important claims are supported by at least one credible source.
- Check dates: make sure information is current and relevant to the question.
- Check that numbers refer to the requested period (e.g. the correct fiscal quarter).
- Resolve conflicting information where possible.
- Clearly identify uncertainty and gaps in the available evidence.
